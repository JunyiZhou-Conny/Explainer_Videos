RL agents for pathology and microscopy images that either choose where to look in gigapixel slides (attention glimpses, PPO observation paths, Q-learning from pathologists' gaze) or produce instance segmentations rewarded by non-differentiable shape priors.

| | Paper | Year | Venue | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| · | [RLogist](reinforcement-learning/rl-for-pathology/zhao2023rlogist.pdf) — Zhao et al. <br><sub>`zhao2023rlogist`</sub> | 2023 | AAAI 2023 | Gigapixel pathology slides are slow to analyse because standard methods extract features from every high-magnification patch, so RLogist trains a PPO agent that scans at low magnification and only zooms into a short path of diagnostically useful regions before classifying the slide. |

## Segmentation · nuclei, cell & gland instances  
`segmentation/nuclei-instance`
Methods for separating and classifying individual nuclei in histology and microscopy images, from shape representations (StarDist polygons, HoVer-Net distance maps) to learning from point clicks only (Qu et al., SC-Net).

| | Paper | Year | Venue | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| · | [DCAN](segmentation/nuclei-instance/chen2016dcan.pdf) — Chen et al. <br><sub>`chen2016dcan`</sub> | 2016 | CVPR 2016 | DCAN separates touching glands by having one network predict both the object region and its contour, then subtracting the contours from the regions; it won the 2015 GlaS gland segmentation challenge. |
| · | [StarDist](segmentation/nuclei-instance/schmidt2018stardist.pdf) — Schmidt et al. <br><sub>`schmidt2018stardist`</sub> | 2018 | MICCAI 2018 | Instead of drawing boxes around cell nuclei or colouring pixels and hoping crowded cells stay apart, StarDist has every pixel predict a star-shaped polygon (its distances to the cell edge along 32 directions) for the nucleus it belongs to, then keeps the best non-overlapping polygons. |
| · | [HoVer-Net](segmentation/nuclei-instance/graham2019hovernet.pdf) — Graham et al. <br><sub>`graham2019hovernet`</sub> | 2019 | Medical Image Analysis 2019 | Cell nuclei in stained tissue images often touch and blur together. HoVer-Net has a network predict, for every pixel, how far it sits horizontally and vertically from the centre of its nucleus, and the sharp jumps in those maps show where one nucleus ends and the next begins; a separate branch then labels each nucleus's cell type. |
| · | [Partial-points nuclei segmentation (Qu et al.)](segmentation/nuclei-instance/qu2020weaknuclei.pdf) — Qu et al. <br><sub>`qu2020weaknuclei`</sub> | 2020 | IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging | Shows that nuclei in tissue images can be segmented when annotators only click the centres of about 10% of them: first train a detector to find all the centres, then grow rough masks from those dots and train a segmentation network on the masks. |
| · | Cellpose ⚠️ *PDF missing* — Stringer et al. <br><sub>`stringer2021cellpose`</sub> | 2021 | Nature Methods 2021 | Cellpose segments cells of almost any shape and imaging type by predicting, for each pixel, which way to flow to reach its cell centre; pixels whose flows end at the same sink belong to the same cell. |
| · | [SC-Net](segmentation/nuclei-instance/lin2023scnet.pdf) — Lin et al. <br><sub>`lin2023scnet`</sub> | 2023 | arXiv preprint | Pathologists can cheaply click one dot per nucleus instead of tracing outlines; SC-Net turns those dots into rough masks, then improves them in two ways: two networks teach each other (co-training), and a self-supervised side task learns to re-colour the hematoxylin (nucleus-stain) channel back into the full H&E image. |

## Segmentation · topology-aware losses  
`segmentation/topology-aware`
Losses and metrics that make a segmentation keep the right connected pieces, holes and branches, using persistent homology, soft skeletons (clDice) and Betti matching.

| | Paper | Year | Venue | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| · | [Topological prior loss (Clough et al.)](segmentation/topology-aware/clough2019topological.pdf) — Clough et al. <br><sub>`clough2019topological`</sub> | 2019 | arXiv preprint | Lets you tell a segmentation network what shape type to expect, e.g. 'the heart muscle in this view is a ring: one connected piece with one hole'. It penalises predictions whose topology differs, using persistent homology to make the count of pieces and holes differentiable. |
| · | [TopoLoss (Hu et al.)](segmentation/topology-aware/hu2019topoloss.pdf) — Hu et al. <br><sub>`hu2019topoloss`</sub> | 2019 | NeurIPS 2019 | Adds a differentiable loss based on persistent homology that pushes a segmentation network to produce the same number of connected components and holes (Betti numbers) as the ground truth, fixing broken vessels and merged membranes. |
| · | [clDice](segmentation/topology-aware/shit2021cldice.pdf) — Shit et al. <br><sub>`shit2021cldice`</sub> | 2021 | arXiv preprint | For thin branching structures like blood vessels, roads or neurons, staying connected matters more than pixel overlap. clDice scores a segmentation by how much of each mask's centreline (skeleton) lies inside the other mask, and a differentiable 'soft' version trains networks not to break these structures apart. |
| · | [Betti Matching](segmentation/topology-aware/stucki2023barcodes.pdf) — Stucki et al. <br><sub>`stucki2023barcodes`</sub> | 2023 | ICML 2023 | Pixel-overlap losses like Dice can't tell whether a segmentation has the right number of connected pieces and loops in the right places, so this paper uses a tool from algebraic topology (induced matchings of persistence barcodes) to pair each topological feature in the prediction with the spatially corresponding feature in the ground truth, yielding both a metric and a trainable loss. |
| · | [Multi-class Betti Matching](segmentation/topology-aware/berger2024topo.pdf) — Berger et al. <br><sub>`berger2024topo`</sub> | 2024 | arXiv preprint | Extends the Betti matching topology loss from yes/no (binary) segmentation to images with many labeled structures, such as heart chambers or artery vs. vein, by treating each class as its own binary map so that connected pieces and holes come out right for every class. |
