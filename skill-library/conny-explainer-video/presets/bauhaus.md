# Bauhaus / geometric composition and Dessau print

Source: 路北路陈's [Bauhaus episode](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1FGpg6sECA/). Its central distinction is useful: the historical school, recognizable Dessau-era printing, and today's geometric shorthand are different things. Full-timeline visual sheets and the creator's chapter/style description inform this preset. Do not present a red/yellow/blue poster as the only historical Bauhaus aesthetic.

## Choose the relevant mode

Default `geometric`: use for elementary concepts, transformations, decomposition, composition, and clear visual metaphors. A few large circles, squares, triangles, half-circles and quarter-circles organize the frame. Flat primary accents plus black and warm paper; generous empty space; asymmetry; sans-serif type treated as a shape. Not every frame needs all three primary colors. Rotation is allowed when it serves the composition.

`dessau-print`: use when the user asks for historical printing or an editorial documentary. Warm paper, mainly black, one or two red marks, bold lowercase sans-serif where appropriate, heavy black bars, asymmetric left-aligned blocks, dramatic type scale, and monochrome imagery. Do not select a font merely because its name is “Bauhaus.” The creator specifically distinguishes a later rounded display face from earlier printing.

## Original implementation tokens

At 1920×1080: paper `#F0EBDD`, ink `#161616`, red `#D94332`, yellow `#E8BE32`, blue `#3159A5`. Headings 88–128 px; body 40–48 px; diagram labels at least 30 px. Use an available OFL geometric/grotesk sans and Noto Sans CJK SC. Captions stay upright in a quiet bottom 170 px band, Chinese above English. These values are our production choices, not measured source tokens.

## Motion and teaching

Build an object from its parts while explaining their roles. Split a circle into two halves to teach division; transfer a rectangle from one group to another to teach ownership; let an aligned bar establish the axis for a new comparison. Use 0.5–1.0 s construction moves, then hold long enough to understand the result. Use a different meaningful transformation when the idea changes; do not rotate shapes continuously as decoration.

For a skills explainer: use a square for the instruction, a circle for the agent, and a triangle for a tool; show the three combining into a working process. Label them explicitly so shape/color never carries meaning alone. Keep that mapping stable throughout the episode.

For Dessau mode: enlarge one typographic statement, slide a heavy rule into alignment, then reveal the diagram or source that supports the statement. Retain the page's spatial organization between details and overview.

## Distinctions to preserve

- De Stijl's orthogonal partitions and rectangular planes differ from circles and triangles assembled as large shapes.
- Swiss design uses an editorial alignment grid and restrained typography; it need not advertise its shapes.
- Memphis-like squiggles, confetti and arbitrary disruption are outside this preset.
- Flat teaching shapes do not need gradients, bevels or shadows. Subtle shadows on an explicitly depicted historical paper photograph are a separate material choice.

## Acceptance

Each large shape must explain a relationship or support hierarchy. Keep space for captions; avoid ornamental text rotation for sentences. In the brief, record `geometric` or `dessau-print`.
