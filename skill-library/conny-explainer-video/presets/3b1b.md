# Dark mathematical diagrams (`3b1b`)

Use for mathematics, algorithms, quantitative intuition and research papers. This is the
repository's existing 3Blue1Brown-inspired teaching approach, not an affiliation, an exact
reproduction or a clone of the creator's narration. Preserve the existing episodes and toolkit.

## Visual and teaching grammar

Show an object, change it, then introduce the equation that explains what the viewer saw.
Build one idea per narration beat. Carry semantic colors through the entire episode: a
variable, probability or data structure keeps its identity. Near-black `#0D0F14` background,
off-white `#ECECEC` text, teal/blue for primary objects, yellow for attention and red for
counterexamples follow the current `explainer/style.py` defaults. Use CMU Serif for mathematical
labels and legible CJK type for Chinese text. Let equations transform while matching terms
retain their position or color. Avoid a slide deck filled with bullet points.

Introduce a small concrete example before generalization. Include a pause-and-ponder beat,
the most likely misconception, and a recap that reconnects the example to the general rule.
For paper episodes, maintain evidence links to pages and equations and add exercises.

## Implementation

The checkout's `docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md` contains detailed sizes, spacing and motion conventions;
`docs/WORKFLOW.md` describes the original `video.yaml` production route. The existing
`explainer/style.py`, `components.py` and `scene.py` implement this family. Reuse them when they
fit the explanation. Existing `video.yaml` episodes keep their own 1080p60 specifications;
selecting this preset for a new brief does not override the requested duration or frame rate.

Completed examples include `videos/dwork2006-calibrating-noise` and `videos/tictactoe-255168`.
The new `episode.json` runner is also usable with newly authored dark scenes, but no automatic
conversion between the two episode formats is provided.

## Acceptance

The viewer can identify the active object, follow the transformation, and explain why the
equation applies. Captions remain readable and clear of diagrams. A sample must test a real
explanation, not only a title card. Keep lesson facts separate from visual inspiration.
