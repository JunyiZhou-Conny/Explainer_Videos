# Swiss / 瑞士国际主义平面设计

Use for clear technical explanations, folder maps, processes, numbers and comparisons. This is an original production preset informed by 路北路陈's [Swiss episode](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1hWar6SEVP/), especially the grid demonstration at 00:55–01:15, typography at 01:25–01:50, alignment at 01:55–02:15, and final style card at 03:50–04:05. Local visual review covers the full timeline through timestamped frames; observations and adopted production values are separate below.

## Visual grammar

- Warm off-white field, near-black type, one confident red accent. Reference uses flat fills, thin rules, oversized headings and small editorial identifiers.
- A modular grid organizes both dense diagrams and large empty areas. Align by visible edges and baselines. Asymmetry should feel balanced, never arbitrary.
- Sans-serif text; use weight and scale to create hierarchy. Keep text left aligned with a ragged right edge. Use actual paths in a monospace face only when exact spelling matters.
- Every diagram must teach a relationship. Show an object, label it, then expose its structure. A before/after layout transformation is more useful than a paragraph describing alignment.

## Motion and sequencing

Open with a concrete visual question, then let the answer reorganize the same objects. Prefer connected transformations: scattered folders align into categories; one recipe branches toward three agents; a long paragraph resolves into saved parameters.

Use precise 0.4–0.8 second entrances and 0.8–1.5 second explanatory transformations, followed by enough reading time. Use cubic easing without rubbery bounce. Alternate close detail, a whole diagram, and occasional typographic chapter cards. Let the accent follow the current point. Reveal grid guides only while explaining structure, then remove them.

Choose transitions from a small varied vocabulary: edge-aligned slide, shared-object transform, line wipe, camera crop/zoom, hard cut with a matching shape. Avoid repeating the same transition every scene. Keep continuous motion purposeful; do not animate text merely to fill narration time.

## Do not

No decorative gradients, soft dashboard cards everywhere, glass blur, fake 3D, random rotation, persistent grid wallpaper, or red used on every label. Do not duplicate the creator's posters, script, logo or exact scene sequence. Sparse slides with unchanged text for long narration are not a finished animation.

## Acceptance

A viewer should understand the main relationship with audio muted. Review every scene at full size and a phone-size preview. Check bilingual text for clipping, a consistent caption band, legible paths, and enough empty space. Include at least one animated structural demonstration for each major concept, not only title changes.
